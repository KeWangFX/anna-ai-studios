02 — Pilot scope

What we agree
before a pilot

A pilot starts with one useful workflow and a named owner. The scope below is agreed for your environment before implementation.

Team and first workflow
For creative and technical teams with a repeated task and someone able to review its output. Start with one asset-preparation, knowledge-search or production task; choose the workflow during assessment.
What you prepare
A current workflow example, authorised sample files or documents, expected outputs and a team contact. Confirm access, data permissions and any security restrictions before sharing production material.
Proposed pilot deliverables
The scope should specify a working workflow, its agreed integrations, review controls, example outputs and an operating handover. Source files, deployment access and documentation are listed explicitly in the proposal.
Acceptance checks
Agree a test set, an output-quality threshold, failure and recovery checks, manual-review steps and a usage-cost limit. Compare the result with the current workflow; wider rollout requires a separate decision.
Operation and third-party costs
Agree who owns monitoring, updates, incident handling and account access. You approve provider accounts and budgets; third-party model, telephone and hosting usage is itemised separately.
Assessment and next step
Send the task, current process and desired output through the review request. Scope, timeline, pricing and support arrangements are confirmed in writing after assessment; submitting the form does not confirm a booking or start delivery.
